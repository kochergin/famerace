## 0. Executive conclusion

The original idea — “let anyone invest in any person” — is emotionally powerful, but dangerous if executed literally. In the wrong framing it sounds like “human stocks,” “Messi token without Messi,” “betting on people’s lives,” or “a crypto casino for personal identity.” That immediately creates exposure across securities, gambling, NIL/right-of-publicity, consumer protection, harassment, influencer marketing and trust/safety.

The right version is:

> **FameRace is not a people exchange. It is a career launchpad where fans back rising talent, creators monetize early hype, and communities help make the breakout happen.**

The strongest final formula:

> **Market creates attention. Backstage creates revenue. Missions create life-change. Street Team creates virality. Proof-of-early creates status.**

FameRace should not be just “Pump.fun for personal tokens.” The winning product is:

> **Pump.fun × Polymarket × OnlyFans mechanics × Patreon × talent agency × social game.**

Do **not** publicly call it “OnlyFans for talent.” That creates the wrong adult-content association. But internally, FameRace should absolutely learn from OnlyFans: direct fan monetization, subscriptions, paid drops, tips, paid messages, intimacy, exclusivity, and a clear creator P&L.

The core reframing:

- Not “invest in people.”
- Not “buy human tokens.”
- Not “profit from Messi.”
- Not “trade people.”

Instead:

> **Discover rising talent. Back their rise. Fund their next move. Prove your taste.**

## 0A. V2 upgrade: Explosion Launch System

### 0A.1 The main upgrade

To explode from day one, FameRace cannot launch as an empty marketplace. Empty marketplaces die: not enough creators, not enough liquidity, not enough social proof, not enough reasons to share, and too many users asking “so what?”

The correct launch pattern:

> **Create the market before the market opens.**

Before public trading, FameRace should already have:

- public nominations;
- creator waitrooms;
- fan demand orders;
- refundable pledges or pre-commitments;
- scout pressure;
- launch thresholds;
- creator missions;
- opening demand;
- share cards;
- category rivalries;
- a public scoreboard;
- a reason for creators to claim their profiles.

Day one should not feel like “we launched a website.” It should feel like:

> **The Internet Drafts 100 Future Stars.**

This changes the entire dynamic. Users do not arrive only to buy a token. They arrive to participate in a cultural draft.

### 0A.2 The key trick: Draft Vault

**Draft Vault** is the pre-market demand layer.

Before a creator claims and verifies a profile, FameRace can host a **Draft Profile**. This is not a tradable profile, not a token, not a “human stock,” and not a creator market. It is a public demand signal.

A Draft Profile shows:

- who has been nominated;
- how many fans want to see them on FameRace;
- how much launch demand is waiting;
- which mission fans most want to fund;
- who the scouts are;
- how much invite pressure has been created;
- whether the creator has started the claim flow.

Example:

```text
#12 DRAFT
MIRA
Indie singer · nominated by fans

842 fans waiting
$21,400 pledged demand
Top requested mission: First Music Video
17 scouts inviting
Status: Unclaimed

[Invite MIRA]
[Pledge if claimed]
[Add to roster]
```

The core rule:

> **Anyone can draft. Only verified/claimed creators can go live.**

This preserves the viral promise — “anyone can become a market” — while removing the most toxic element: unauthorized trading of people. A Draft Profile must not include live trading, a price, a market cap, a token, a creator fee, unauthorized commercial likeness, or any misleading implication of endorsement.

### 0A.3 Fan Demand Orders: liquidity before launch

Launchpads often open empty. FameRace should solve this through **Fan Demand Orders**.

A **Fan Demand Order** is a pre-launch intent:

```text
I will back MIRA with $100 if she claims her FameRace launch.
```

Types of intent:

1. **Genesis Pass intent** — I want a Genesis Pass when she launches.
2. **Mission pledge intent** — I want to fund her first mission.
3. **Market buy intent** — I want to place an opening buy after claim.
4. **Backstage intent** — I want to subscribe to Backstage.

Safety and UX rules:

- before claim/verification, this can be non-binding waitlist intent, refundable pledge, or pre-authorization;
- money does not go to the creator until the official launch;
- users receive a confirmation window before final commitment;
- if the creator does not claim, the intent expires or refunds;
- if the launch threshold is not met, the market does not open;
- the UI must not imply the creator endorsed FameRace before claim.

This creates the most important creator-acquisition message:

> “You already have 1,842 fans and $47k of demand waiting. Claim your FameRace launch.”

That is much stronger than a cold pitch.

---

### 0A.4 Opening Batch Auction

When a creator claims the profile and passes the threshold, FameRace should not immediately open a chaotic bonding curve with snipers and unfair fills.

Recommended launch sequence:

1. **Launch announcement** — creator verified, launch in 24–48 hours.
2. **Final confirmation window** — backers confirm pre-launch demand.
3. **Opening batch auction** — confirmed opening orders clear through one fair mechanism.
4. **Genesis allocation** — early backers receive badges, Backer Wall spots, and launch status.
5. **Bonding curve starts** — live price discovery begins after the opening allocation.

Why this matters:

- reduces sniping;
- creates a fairer launch;
- starts the market with real demand;
- gives the creator visible social proof;
- creates a PR moment: “$84k opening demand confirmed.”

Public line:

> **No empty launches. Every market opens with a crowd.**

### 0A.5 Launch Thresholds: do not create dead markets

Not every nominated creator should go live. FameRace needs a **Launch Score** and hard launch gates.

Example Season 1 threshold:

```text
Minimum Launch Threshold:
- creator verified and consented;
- 250 confirmed fans/backers;
- $10,000 confirmed launch demand;
- first mission configured;
- 3 creator perks configured;
- creator launch kit approved;
- trust/safety review passed;
- no minors unless a specific legal/category flow supports them;
- no prohibited content or market framing.
```

If the threshold is not met, the profile stays in Draft or Waitroom.

This creates scarcity:

> **Only demanded creators go live.**

It also solves liquidity fragmentation. Do not launch 1,000 empty markets. Launch 10–20 strong markets on day one, then 3–5 new launches per day.

### 0A.6 Pre-created profiles: yes, but with safe statuses

Pre-created profiles are useful and necessary. They create the sense that FameRace is already alive before day one. But they must be separated by status.

#### Status 1: Draft Stub

Unclaimed. No trading. No token. No price. No market cap. No unauthorized commercial portrait as the main asset.

Allowed:

- public handle or link;
- category;
- reason nominated;
- fan count;
- non-binding demand;
- requested mission;
- invite button;
- claim button;
- takedown button.

#### Status 2: Claim Room

Creator has started the claim flow but is not live yet.

Allowed:

- “claim in progress”;
- creator verification status;
- draft demand;
- initial mission configuration;
- launch checklist;
- private creator dashboard.

#### Status 3: Launching Soon

Creator has verified and approved launch terms.

Allowed:

- launch countdown;
- confirmed perks;
- first mission;
- opening demand window;
- launch kit;
- share cards;
- opening auction schedule.

#### Status 4: Live

Creator has fully launched.

Allowed:

- Genesis Pass / Fame Token;
- bonding curve or market;
- Backstage;
- missions;
- paid drops;
- tips;
- paid messages;
- Street Team quests;
- Backer Wall;
- creator analytics.

### 0A.7 Where liquidity comes from: 7 sources

#### 1. Draft Vault liquidity

The best liquidity is not platform-provided liquidity. It is social demand collected before the market opens.

#### 2. Staggered launches

Do not open 100 live markets at once. Start with a small number of strong launches and create daily events.

Recommended launch rhythm:

```text
Day 1: 5–10 live verified creators
Days 2–7: 3–5 launches per day
Remaining creators: Draft / Launching Soon / Waitroom
```

#### 3. Bonding curve cold-start market

For early markets, a bonding curve is better than a thin order book. Users can buy and sell without needing immediate two-sided depth.

#### 4. FameRace Match Fund

Create a Season 1 **Match Fund** to amplify missions, not to pump tokens.

Example:

```text
$500,000 FameRace Season 1 Match Fund

Used for:
- matching creator missions;
- creator grants;
- scout rewards;
- Street Team prizes;
- category winner bonuses;
- launch campaigns.
```

Best public framing:

> **We are funding 100 rising creators in 30 days.**

Not:

> “We are providing token liquidity.”

#### 5. Market maker / LP rewards after graduation

After a creator graduates from bonding curve to AMM/orderbook, FameRace can offer maker rewards, fee rebates, or points for healthy market depth. Rewards should score tight spreads, sustained uptime, real depth, and organic participation, not wash volume.

#### 6. Creator-owned audience

The creator’s own audience is the cheapest and strongest liquidity source. This is why the product must give creators a real reason to bring fans:

- Genesis Pass primary sale;
- Backstage subscription;
- paid drops;
- tips;
- missions;
- creator fee from market activity;
- public proof that fans funded the next career move.

#### 7. Backer Crews / syndicates

Backers should not act only as individuals. They should organize into crews. Crews can coordinate launches, missions, quests, and category competitions.

This creates team-based liquidity and retention.

- they buy early merch;
- they discover founders before VCs do;
- they promote athletes before the league notices;

## 4. Product architecture

FameRace has six core layers.

### 4.1 Market

The market layer creates attention and price discovery.

Components:

- Genesis Pass / Fame Token;
- bonding curve launch;
- opening batch auction;
- holder count;
- volume;
- market activity feed;
- creator fee;
- graduation to deeper market infrastructure after sufficient activity.

Market language should be careful. The token should be framed as access/status/utility, not equity, debt, revenue share or ownership of the creator.

### 4.2 Backstage

Backstage is the direct-to-fan monetization layer.

Components:

- monthly membership;
- private creator feed;
- behind-the-scenes updates;
- early demos;
- private videos;
- Q&A;
- paid messages;
- paid requests;
- private livestreams;
- holder-only drops;
- community posts.

Backstage gives creators recurring revenue and gives backers a reason to return.

### 4.3 Missions

- fight camp;
- debut EP;
- beta launch;
- tour van;
- art show;
- camera equipment;
- editing budget;
- merch drop;
- local event;

```text
Goal: $12,000

- $10 Mission Badge
```

## 7. Core user flows

### 7.1 Nominate → Claim → Launch

1. User finds rising talent.
2. User creates Draft Profile.
3. Other users vote, pledge, share and invite.
4. Creator receives claim link.
5. Creator verifies identity and social accounts.
6. Creator configures mission, perks and launch kit.
7. Profile reaches Launch Threshold.
8. Launching Soon countdown begins.
9. Opening Batch Auction finalizes demand.
10. Creator goes live.

### 7.2 Back creator

1. User lands on creator profile.
2. User sees story, mission, Fame Score, Backer Wall and market.
3. User clicks “Back.”
4. User selects amount or tier.
5. User sees benefits and risk disclosure.
6. User confirms payment/wallet transaction.
7. User receives Genesis Backer badge / token / receipt.
8. User is prompted to add creator to Roster.
9. User receives share card.

### 7.3 Join Backstage

1. User opens Backstage preview.
2. User sees locked content and membership benefits.
3. User subscribes or qualifies through holding requirement.
4. User unlocks feed.
5. User receives notifications for drops and updates.

### 7.4 Unlock paid drop

1. Creator posts paid drop.
2. Users see preview.
3. User pays or unlocks based on tier.
4. Content unlocks.
5. Creator receives payout.
6. User can share a non-content-revealing card.

### 7.5 Fund Mission

1. Creator creates mission.
2. Mission has goal, deadline, use of funds, tiers and proof requirements.
3. Backer contributes.
4. Progress bar updates live.
5. Mission reaches funding threshold.
6. Creator posts proof and progress updates.
7. Backers receive rewards, badges and status.
8. Outcome becomes a marketing story.

### 7.6 Street Team quest

1. Creator creates quest.
2. Backers complete action.
3. User submits proof or system verifies action.
4. Creator/admin approves or auto-verifies.
5. User receives XP, badge, access, points or boost.
6. Crew leaderboard updates.

### 7.7 Creator payout

1. Creator earns from primary sales, subscriptions, drops, tips, messages, requests and fees.
2. Dashboard shows available balance, pending balance and payout history.
3. Creator requests payout or follows scheduled payout.
4. Compliance/fraud checks run.
5. Payout sent via supported rails.
6. Creator receives reporting and export.

## 15. Compliance, legal and safety guardrails

This section is not legal advice. It is product guidance for counsel and operators.

### 15.1 Consent-only live profiles

Core rule:

> **No live markets without creator consent and verification.**

Draft Profiles may show demand, but they must not trade or imply endorsement.

No unauthorized:

- celebrity tokens;
- athlete tokens;
- creator market caps;
- commercial use of name/likeness;
- fake endorsement;
- profile monetization.

Every creator must have:

- claim flow;
- terms acceptance;
- payout setup;
- rights confirmation;
- category review.

### 15.2 No investment-contract language

Avoid all language that suggests users profit from the creator’s future efforts.

Do not promise:

- returns;
- revenue share;
- equity;
- debt;
- royalties;
- creator income participation;
- guaranteed upside.

Frame the product as:

- access;
- status;
- support;
- perks;
- missions;
- participation;
- proof-of-early.

### 15.3 Required disclosures

User-facing flows must include:

- token/Pass is not equity;
- not debt;
- not revenue share;
- not ownership of creator;
- price can go down;
- creator may stop posting;
- perks are subject to terms;
- missions carry fulfillment risk;
- paid content rules;
- refund policy;
- creator fee disclosure;
- platform fee disclosure.

### 15.4 Prohibited markets and content

Prohibit:

- death;
- injury;
- illness;
- relapse;
- divorce;
- arrest;
- crime allegations;
- sexual allegations;
- harassment;
- doxxing;
- hate;
- minors without special flow;
- private persons without public profile;
- cancellation markets;
- war/terror/assassination;
- self-harm;
- exploitation;
- defamatory claims.

FameRace must not become a place where users bet on personal tragedy.

### 15.5 Prediction markets

Prediction/milestone markets should be delayed until legal structure is clear.

Potential approaches:

- free-to-play points;
- non-cash badges;
- regulated partner;
- geo-fenced compliant product;
- limited categories;
- strict prohibited market policy.

### 15.6 Influencer marketing

If creators or influencers receive material benefits, disclosures must be built into launch content.

Launch Kit must include disclosure templates.

### 15.7 Minors

Default rule:

> **No minors in live markets at V1.**

If later allowed, it requires:

- guardian consent;
- NIL counsel;
- category-specific rules;
- stricter moderation;
- no trading if not legally supported;
- no sensitive markets;
- extra payout controls.

### 15.8 Anti-rug design

Risks:

- creator launches, collects money, disappears;
- fake creators;
- hidden allocations;
- misleading promises;
- abandoned missions.

Mitigations:

- creator verification;
- no huge unlocked creator premine;
- transparent fees;
- vesting if allocations exist;
- public creator wallet labels;
- mission proof requirements;
- payout holds for high-risk cases;
- creator reputation;
- refund/dispute rules;
- abandoned creator labels.

### 15.9 Trust and safety systems

Required:

- impersonation detection;
- duplicate profile detection;
- takedown flow;
- harassment reporting;
- content moderation;
- mission review;
- payout risk review;
- bot/wash trading detection;
- market pause;
- user blocking;
- creator suspension;
- audit logs.

## 22. Risks and mitigations

### 22.1 Legal/regulatory risk

Risk: product perceived as securities/gambling.

Mitigation:

- avoid investment language;
- no revenue-share claims;
- access/status/utility framing;
- legal review;
- geo controls if needed;
- prediction markets delayed.

### 22.2 Right of publicity / NIL risk

Risk: unauthorized commercial use of identity.

Mitigation:

- no live market without consent;
- Draft Profiles only as demand signals;
- claim/takedown flow;
- legal review for categories;
- no unauthorized celebrity tokens.

### 22.3 Harassment/private-life risk

Risk: users create harmful markets/content.

Mitigation:

- prohibited categories;
- moderation;
- reporting;
- takedowns;
- no personal tragedy markets;
- no private-life speculation.

### 22.4 Creator rug risk

Risk: creator collects money and disappears.

Mitigation:

- mission proof requirements;
- payout holds;
- reputation;
- transparent status;
- refund/dispute policy;
- creator update requirements.

### 22.5 Wash trading and manipulation

Risk: fake volume and pump schemes.

Mitigation:

- bot detection;
- wash-trading detection;
- limits;
- reward rules not based purely on volume;
- suspicious activity review;
- market pause.

### 22.6 Creator supply risk

Risk: creators do not want to launch.

Mitigation:

- lead with missions and revenue;
- show demand before asking;
- creator-friendly fees;
- launch kit;
- hero creator case studies.

### 22.7 Demand risk

Risk: users visit once and leave.

Mitigation:

- Roster;
- Taste Score;
- weekly leagues;
- missions;
- Backstage;
- Street Team;
- crews;
- live feed;
- share cards.

### 22.8 Brand risk

Risk: FameRace seen as “crypto casino on people.”

Mitigation:

- mission-first messaging;
- consent-only markets;
- brand-safe categories;
- no celebrity exploitation;
- no tragedy markets;
- Electric Backstage design, not casino design.
